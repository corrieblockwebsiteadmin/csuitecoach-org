How this list differs

This list applies the same criteria and the same weights as its global sister ranking, unchanged, to a defined field. It is not a lower bar and it is not a separate standard. It exists because the general rankings under-represent women relative to their actual presence in the profession, and because a list that has to reach for its third woman is telling you something about the list rather than about the field.

A large share of recognition in executive coaching originates from one connected network — Thinkers50's coaching award bears Marshall Goldsmith's name, its Coaching Legends programme runs with his 100 Coaches network, and many honoured coaches belong to it. Counting a Legend induction, a Coaches50 listing, network membership and a personal endorsement as four independent validations is how a small circle comes to look like a consensus. That network is counted once here, at the level of its strongest single element, and recognition from genuinely separate sources — peer-reviewed publication, university presses, professional-body governance — is weighted higher.

Several widely-repeated claims are contradicted by their own sources and are not printed. A "#1 in leadership by Global Gurus" title held in 2024 was #3 by 2025 and absent from the 2026 list. A Thinkers50 award described as won was a longlist nomination. An ICF "Global Board Chair" was in fact Global President, a different office, and the body chaired was the ICF Foundation. Where a coach's own materials and the awarding body disagree, the awarding body is followed.

Disclosed gap. Five of ten are US-based, and that concentration is a property of the recognition bodies rather than of the search. Reading all fifty Thinkers50 Coaches50 entries and both Global Gurus coaching lists turns up no woman based in Latin America, East Asia or the Gulf. A full sweep of ICF's 2026 and 2025 global board rosters across 25 countries found exactly one Latin American member, and he is male. Reducing the US share further would mean lowering the evidence bar rather than searching harder — so it is disclosed instead, and Latin America is budgeted for a dedicated research pass before the February edition.

1

Sanyin Siang

Durham, North Carolina, USA

Executive Director, Coach K Center on Leadership & Ethics, Duke University Fuqua School of Business; Professor, Duke Pratt School of Engineering

She holds the strongest award record of any woman in this field: recipient of the Thinkers50 Distinguished Achievement Award for Coaching & Mentoring in 2019 — a won award decided by a panel, not a listing, a longlist or a vote — plus a Coaching Legends induction and three appearances on the Thinkers50 Ranked Thinkers list. Under the interlink adjustment that network counts once, and even counted once it is the highest single credential here.

2

Dr Carol Kauffman

United States

Founder, Institute of Coaching, Harvard Medical School; Senior Leadership Advisor, Egon Zehnder

Founding the field's peer-reviewed journal is a different order of contribution from being ranked in it. A journal outlives the person who started it, creates a venue for everyone else's work, and is the single clearest act of generosity anywhere in this study — the criterion this methodology weights at fifteen per cent and most rankings ignore entirely.

3

Sally Helgesen

Chatham, New York, USA

Executive coach, author and speaker on women's leadership

Eight books across Hachette and Berrett-Koehler is the largest body of published work on this list, and it is sustained rather than clustered — a four-decade record of returning to the same problem and revising the answer, which is exactly the development-over-time marker the methodology looks for.

4

Dr Marcia Reynolds

Arizona, USA

President, Covisioning LLC; fifth global President of the International Coaching Federation

Five books with Berrett-Koehler — Breakthrough Coaching (2024), Coach the Person, Not the Problem (2020), The Discomfort Zone (2014), Wander Woman (2010) — is a sustained relationship with a serious independent publisher, and Coach the Person, Not the Problem is used as training material by other coaches rather than only read by clients. Writing what other practitioners teach from is the strongest form of the generosity criterion.

5

Tracy Sinclair

South Croydon, England, UK

Executive and board-level coach; Chair of the ICF Global Board of Directors, 2018

ICF named her a Distinguished Coach in 2023, and that honour sits entirely outside the Thinkers50 and Goldsmith network — under the interlink adjustment, independence of source is precisely what raises a credential's weight. Chairing ICF's global board is the profession's most senior elected office, and she reached it on a peer vote rather than a nomination form.

6

Jennifer Garvey Berger

France

Co-founder and CEO, Cultivating Leadership; author of five books with Stanford University Press

Five books with Stanford University Press is the most rigorous publication record on this list. A university press applies peer review and academic editorial standards that no trade publisher does, and under a rubric weighting published work at thirty per cent with recency and originality both counted, that is worth more than a larger stack of trade titles.

7

Janet M. Harvey

Washington State, USA

CEO, inviteCHANGE; global President of the International Coaching Federation, 2012

ICF MCC plus the global presidency of the profession's largest body is senior standing earned by peer election, entirely independent of the Thinkers50 and Goldsmith network. Leading an accredited training organisation means her contribution compounds through the coaches she certifies rather than stopping at her own client list.

8

Nankhonde Kasonde-van den Broek

Lusaka, Zambia

Founder and CEO, ZANGA African Metrics; executive coach and change practitioner

Recipient of the Thinkers50 Distinguished Achievement Award for Coaching and Mentoring in 2021 — a won, panel-decided award, confirmed on Thinkers50's own site — followed by a Coaching Legends induction in 2023. Only two women in this entire study have actually won that award.

9

Haesun Moon

Toronto, Canada

Executive coach and communication scientist; founder, Canadian Centre for Brief Coaching

The Dialogic Orientation Quadrant is original, published work with an empirical basis — a model of what happens inside a coaching conversation, derived from analysing conversations rather than asserted from experience. That is the engagement-with-evidence marker, and very little coaching writing meets it.

10

Alisa Cohn

New York, USA

Executive coach to founders and C-suite leaders; executive coach, Cornell's NYC tech incubator

From Start-Up to Grown-Up (Kogan Page, 2021) is a genuinely operable manual for founder-CEOs rather than a book about the author — specific, transferable and drawn from practice, which is three of the six thought-leadership markers.

Ones to Watch

Named, not ranked. Practitioners whose work is strong and whose published corpus is still building. This is a different question from the ranking above, not a lower tier of it.

Brenda Bence

Singapore and Las Vegas, Nevada

Thinkers50 Coaching Legend 2025, first entry on the Coaches50 list, and the highest-ranked woman on the Global Gurus coaching list in both 2025 and 2026. A C-suite succession practice that Thinkers50's independent profile describes as spanning executives from 60 nationalities across 70 industries.

  • Her book count is stated as 11 or 12 depending on the page, and no publisher could be verified for any of them. Performance statistics on her site — a 100% succession success rate, 97% client re-engagement, 50+ awards — are self-reported and unaudited, and are excluded. She is also frequently described as Switzerland-based, for which no evidence exists.

Tasha Eurich

Denver, Colorado, USA

Organisational psychologist and researcher whose work on self-awareness reached a genuinely large audience. Three books across Crown, Greenleaf and Pan Macmillan, a Thinkers50 Coaches50 listing and a Thinkers50 Radar appearance in 2019.

  • Held back on verification rather than substance — several headline claims in her materials could not be corroborated at source, and her degrees name no issuing institution. Her publication record is strong enough that resolving those would likely move her into the ten in February.

Atchara Juicharern

Bangkok, Thailand

Executive coach and founder of AcComm & Image International, building coaching capacity across South-East Asia — a region almost entirely absent from the international recognition bodies. Thinkers50 Coaches50 listing and 100 Coaches membership.

  • A "Thinkers50 Marshall Goldsmith Distinguished Achievement Award 2021" appears in her materials. She was a longlist nominee — not shortlisted, not the winner. A Goldsmith-attributed "#1 Coach in Asia" title has no awarding body or methodology behind it. Her regional contribution is real; the award framing is not.

Vijayalakshmi S

India

ICF MCC and ACTC, Secretary/Treasurer of the ICF Global Board, and named an ICF Distinguished Coach in 2024 — senior governance standing in the profession's largest body, and one of very few Indian coaches to hold it.

  • No published books, and no findable personal website — a reader cannot reach her or read her. Both are material gaps under a rubric weighting published work at thirty per cent, and both are straightforwardly fixable before February.

Considered and not included

A ranking that quietly omits well-known names invites the assumption that it did not know about them. These were assessed and left out for stated reasons.

Hortense le Gentil

The Unlocked Leader (Wiley, 2023) and Aligned (Page Two, 2019), with regular Harvard Business Review contribution — a real publication record. Not included this edition because too much of her stated recognition does not survive checking: a "#5 Global Gurus" placement is contradicted by the current source, which shows #27 on the 2026 leadership list and no placement at all on the coaching list; an Inc. listing is a named columnist's opinion piece published under Inc.'s own disclaimer, not an Inc. ranking; and a 2023 Thinkers50 award nomination could not be verified. Her book endorsements from Ralph Lauren, the Best Buy CEO and the Levi Strauss President are jacket blurbs, not clients, and are read as such.

Jihane Labib

ICF MCC and ACTC and Chair of the ICF Professional Coaches Global Board for 2026 — the strongest institutional position held by any coach in the Gulf, and she would qualify for this list comfortably. She is ranked first on the GCC list instead, where a UAE and Morocco-based practice building accredited coach training in an under-served region is the more useful placement. Appearing on both would have been defensible; the sister ranking is where the entry does more work.

Dr Ruth Gotian

A genuine Thinkers50 Radar Award (2021) and two books with Kogan Page and FT/Pearson. Excluded on seniority tier rather than quality: her own coaching page describes selecting "a limited number of mid and high-level professionals," and her expertise is mentorship and high-achiever psychology in academic medicine. Ranking her as a C-suite executive coach would overstate what she says about herself. She holds an Ed.D., not a PhD, contrary to several secondary sources.

Verification still outstanding

Published openly rather than quietly resolved, because a methodology that claims to source everything should show what it has not yet sourced.

  • Latin America — a full sweep of ICF's 2026 and 2025 global board rosters across 25 countries found one Latin American member, and he is male. A dedicated Spanish and Portuguese-language research pass is budgeted before the February edition rather than padding the list now.
  • East Asia and the Gulf — no woman from either region appears on the Thinkers50 Coaches50 list at all. Regional recognition bodies need reading directly.
  • Publisher verification for Brenda Bence's books, Haesun Moon's two unverified titles, and Janet Harvey's
  • Issuing institutions for the doctorates and master's degrees held by Kauffman, Reynolds, Cohn and Eurich — none is named on any page found, including their own
  • Working websites for Sanyin Siang and Haesun Moon, whose domains currently serve no content to a reader
